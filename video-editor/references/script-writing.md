# Writing the script — hook first, story in the middle, one clear call to action

A short video is won or lost in the first 2 seconds and remembered by its last line. This file is the method. The output is `<work>/script.md`.

## Two modes — decide from the first words

| Mode | When | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| **Rhythm** (default) | News, a tip, an offer, anything that must hold attention to the last second | hook → lead (carries the viewer past **second 10**) → 2 × [body + curiosity loop] → bridge → one CTA, with delivery marks |
| **Yapping** (talk to camera) | An opinion, a personal experience, "let me just talk", "no hook", "bullet points only" | one natural opening sentence → 3–5 bullets in their own words → an open-ended closing question. **No manufactured hook, no delivery marks.** Full rules in §8 |

A request like "write me a video about X" with no details = a short rhythm-mode reel.

## 0. What you need to know (ask once, in one message — guess the rest)

| Need | Default if they don't say |
|---|---|
| **Topic / offer** | — (the only thing you must have) |
| **Goal** — sell · get leads · teach · grow followers · announce | grow followers |
| **Audience** — who, and what they already feel | infer from the topic, say it back in one line |
| **Platform** — Reels / TikTok / Shorts / YouTube / LinkedIn | vertical 9:16 short |
| **Length** | 30–45 s |
| **On camera, or voice only?** | voice only |
| **The action** they want at the end (follow, comment a word, DM, link, buy) | follow + comment a word |

Say your reading of it back in one sentence ("A 40-second vertical video that teaches freelancers one pricing mistake, ending with 'comment PRICE'") and go. Don't interview them.

## 1. The three parts

### Part 1 — The hook (first 1–3 seconds, ≤ 12 words)

**Rules**
- **Start in the middle.** No greeting, no "today I'm going to…", no name, no "in this video". The first word already carries the point.
- **One promise or one tension** — never both a list and a story.
- Spoken in the same words the viewer would say to themselves.
- If it still works with the first sentence deleted, delete the first sentence.

**Pick a formula (write 3 hooks, each from a different formula, let the user choose):**

| Formula | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Bold claim** | A true thing most people believe the opposite of | "Your editing isn't why nobody watches." |
| **Stop doing** | Names the mistake and the cost | "Stop starting videos with your name." |
| **Number + result** | A specific, believable number | "Three seconds decide if they stay." |
| **Open question** | A question the viewer can't answer yet | "Why do the best videos start in the middle?" |
| **Before → after** | Shows the jump, hides the how | "Zero views to 40,000 — one change." |
| **Secret / insider** | What the pros do and don't say | "Editors never tell you this about captions." |
| **Direct address** | Calls out the exact person | "If you sell online and post daily, listen." |
| **Pattern break** | Odd, concrete, visual | "This is a $0 camera." |

**The screen hook (second hook, same moment).** Every reel has two hooks at once: the **spoken** one and a **screen hook** — text shown in the very first frame, **6 words or fewer**, different from the spoken line, with a number if possible ("33% never reread").

**One human need per hook** (pick one; mixing two blurs it):

| Need | Trigger | Hook shape |
|---|---|---|
| Certainty / safety | fear of losing or getting it wrong | "Don't do this before you know…" · "Your money leaves while you do nothing" |
| Variety / surprise | the unexpected | "I expected the opposite… it was stranger" |
| Significance | feeling like one of the few who know | "99% don't know this" · "9 in 10 do it wrong" |
| Connection | belonging | "If someone close to you struggles with…, this is for them" |
| Growth | getting better by the end | "After a week of this habit, your work runs twice as well" |
| Contribution | giving something to others | "This isn't for you — it's for your team" |

Rules: (1) one need per hook; (2) the **bridge** lands on growth or contribution — that's what makes people save and share; (3) the **CTA serves the same need** as the hook (connection → "send it to someone who needs it"; significance → "follow for part 2"; growth → "try it today and tell me what happened"; contribution → "send it to your team"); (4) don't use fear in every script — safety and significance are the easiest and strongest, so generators drift to them; **rotate the trigger**. Write the first two lines of every script as `Need: <name>` and `Screen hook: <≤6 words>`.

**26 hook formulas** (fill the `{}` with something specific; each shows how it breaks):

| # | Formula | Template | Breaks when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Confess the loss | "{exact amount} — that's what {one mistake} cost me." | the loss is vague |
| 2 | Order to stop | "Stop {common habit}. Do {alternative} instead." | nobody actually does the habit |
| 3 | Nobody tells you | "Nobody tells you {uncomfortable truth}." | everybody already says it |
| 4 | The swap | "{This} replaces {the expensive thing} for {price}." | the substitute is oversold |
| 5 | Time collapse | "It used to take {long}. Now {short}." | the ratio is unbelievable |
| 6 | The receipts | "I did {thing} for {N days}. These are the real numbers." | no screen recording |
| 7 | Wrong and right | "You're doing {thing} wrong, and it's not your fault. {Fix}." | the "not your fault" is missing |
| 8 | Inside leak | "I spent {N years} {inside}. This is what we never said." | the secret reads like a brochure |
| 9 | Steal it | "Steal {this}. It took me {time} to get right." | the thing isn't on screen |
| 10 | If you're… | "If {specific situation}, this {time} fixes it." | the situation is generic |
| 11 | Number with a favourite | "{N} {things} that {result}. Number {k} nobody uses." | N is above 7 |
| 12 | The objection | "'{objection, word for word}'. Fine. This version works anyway." | the objection is invented |
| 13 | Before / after | "{Old}. {New}. One reason." | three reasons |
| 14 | The call-out | "Hey {narrow group}, this is what you ignore." | the group is wide |
| 15 | Failure log | "The first {N} did nothing. This changed it on number {N+1}." | the change looks like luck |
| 16 | The literal question | "'{question exactly as asked}' — I get it every week." | the question was cleaned up |
| 17 | Head to head | "{A} vs {B}. I tried both for {time}; one wasn't close." | a diplomatic ending |
| 18 | Start mid-demo | "{mid-action} — watch what it does." | the payoff arrives after 12 s |
| 19 | The deadline | "{Thing} changes on {date}. Do {action} before then." | the urgency is fake |
| 20 | Permission | "You don't need {what everyone says you need}." | the obstacle is removed with no alternative |
| 21 | Mid-sentence | "…and that's where {the turn} happened." | you never fill the gap |
| 22 | Flip the saying | "{well-known saying, reversed}." | nobody knows the saying |
| 23 | The statistic | "{N}% of {group} {surprising fact}." | no source |
| 24 | The reveal | "This is the new {thing}." | the reveal has no stake |
| 25 | Someone else's result | "One {person} {got the result} in {time}." | no name, no link |
| 26 | Absolute superlative | "The {fastest/biggest/only} way to {X} is {answer}." | the answer is soft |

Universal rules: no preamble · say the number ("a lot" is not a number) · the first frame is the hook · if it would work for anyone in your field, it isn't your hook · the hook is a promise that pays off before the middle of the video. **Speak from the viewer's side, never the company's** — "a company launches a feature" is the weakest hook; say what changes for the viewer.

Never promise what the video doesn't deliver (it kills retention in the second half and the account's trust).

### Part 2 — The story (the body)

Use one of these skeletons. **One idea per video.** One sentence = one idea = one breath (6–14 words).

| Skeleton | Beats | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| **PAS** | Problem → Agitate (what it costs) → Solution | Ads, offers |
| **Mistake → Fix → Proof** | The mistake · why it hurts · the fix in 1–3 steps · a result | Teaching, authority |
| **Story arc** | Normal → Trouble → Turn → Result → Lesson | Personal, trust |
| **Myth → Truth** | The myth · why it's wrong · the truth · what to do | Contrarian, shares |
| **List with a twist** | 3 items, the third is the surprise | Saves, quick tips |
| **Demo** | Result first → how in 3 steps → result again | Products, apps |

**Rhythm structure (the default).** Hook (0–3 s) → **Lead** (3–10 s) → two cycles of [body + open loop] → bridge (last 6–3 s) → CTA (last 3 s).
- **The 10-second mark is critical:** platforms read retention at second 10 as a strong distribution signal. The lead must pin the viewer *before* that mark — reassure them the hook wasn't a trap, hint without revealing, and give no "okay, I get it, I'll scroll" moment.
- **Exactly two cycles** (value → curiosity → value → curiosity). It gives each idea a clear start and end instead of an endless pulse. Every paragraph ends by tying into the next — no natural exit point.
- Body units of ~3 s, rotating among: a hanging question · a number · a contradiction ("but there's something more important") · a promise of what's coming ("I'll tell you the secret in a moment").
- A technical or complex topic: simplify the core fact, keep the rhythm unchanged. Recent news or numbers: verify them by search before writing.

**Delivery marks** (optional; they let the speaker record with the same rhythm — offer a clean copy without them for publishing):

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `[beat]` | a short pause before the next word |
| `[burst]` | the next word louder and faster |
| `...` | half a second of held breath |
| new line | a separate camera cut or angle change |

If the script is voiced over footage from another video, put a divider line before each part — `🟢 clip N behind (X s)` — it's a separator, not spoken text, and the part's length must fit X.

**Retention tools**
- **Open a loop** in the hook and close it only at the end ("…and the third one surprised me").
- **Re-hook every 8–10 s:** a turn, a number, a "but", a question. A flat 15-second stretch loses people.
- **Concrete over abstract:** a name, a number, a place, an object beats a concept.
- **Show, don't explain:** every line should be able to have a picture (see §3).
- Cut every sentence that doesn't move the viewer one step closer to the promise.

### Part 3 — The call to action (last 2–4 seconds)

- **One action only.** Two asks = no ask.
- **Direct and specific:** "Comment PRICE and I'll send it", "Follow for one tip a day", "Link in my bio" — never "if you liked it, maybe…".
- **Give a reason** for the action ("so you don't miss part 2").
- **Match the goal:** sell → link/DM · leads → comment a word · grow → follow · teach → save/share.
- Optional **micro-CTA** at ~60% ("save this for later") only if the video is 45 s+. Never in the first 5 s.
- The CTA is also the **end card** text. Replace the engine's default end card (it is an ad for this skill) with the user's own: set `theme.json → outro`.

## 2. Length and pace

| Language | Words per second (natural speech) | 30 s | 45 s | 60 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| English | 2.5 | ~75 | ~110 | ~150 |
| Arabic / French / Spanish / Portuguese | 2.2–2.4 | ~68 | ~100 | ~135 |

Count the words, don't guess. Over budget → cut, don't speed up the voice.

## 3. Write for the eye too (the shot list)

Next to every line, decide what the viewer **sees**. This is what makes the film hybrid instead of all-graphics:

| Visual | When | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `person` | The speaker on camera (hook and CTA are best on camera) | their footage |
| `graphic` | A number, a comparison, a step, a diagram, a word being emphasised | the motion engine |
| `image` | A concrete object / place / person / mood | stock → Gemini → Flow (`references/stock.md`, `references/gen-scenes.md`) |
| `clip` | A real action or a place that moves | stock clip → Flow |
| `screen` | A website or app | `references/web-app-explainer.md` |

Rule of thumb: **never more than ~4 seconds of the same visual type in a row**; the hook line and the CTA line each get their own visual.


## 5. Checklist before showing it (run it silently, fix, then show)

- [ ] `Need:` and `Screen hook:` (≤ 6 words, different from the spoken line) are written; the CTA serves the same need.
- [ ] Rhythm mode: the lead carries the viewer past second 10, and there are exactly two body+loop cycles.
- [ ] The first word is not a greeting, a name or "today".
- [ ] The hook makes a promise the body keeps.
- [ ] One idea. One CTA. The CTA is the last spoken thing.
- [ ] Every sentence ≤ 14 words and can be said in one breath.
- [ ] Numbers and names are real — nothing invented. If a fact needs a source, mark it `[source?]` and ask.
- [ ] Word count fits the length.
- [ ] Spoken text has no stage directions, no emoji, no parentheses (numbers written as the voice should say them).
- [ ] No two same-type visuals in a row for more than ~4 s.

Then show: **the 3 hooks first (let them pick), then the full script.** One round of edits is normal; apply them and re-check the checklist.

## 6. If they bring their own script
Don't rewrite it. Run the checklist and report only what fails ("Your first line is a greeting — here are 3 stronger openings. The CTA asks for two things — keep 'comment PRICE'"). Apply only what they approve.

## 7. Adding another writing method
If the user installs another script-writing skill they like (a storytelling framework, a hook library), use it for Parts 1–3 and still finish with §3–§5 here, so the script always leaves with a shot list and the checklist done.

## 8. Yapping mode (talk to camera, bullets, no manufactured hook)

The viewer should feel they're sitting with a friend, not watching an ad. Retention comes from **honesty and closeness**, not engineered curiosity. So the script is **bullets to lean on**, not text to read word for word. (Fully improvised is worse: ideas get lost and the video drags. The middle path: the idea is written, the wording is theirs on the day.)

```
Opening (0–4 s)   one natural sentence about what they'll talk about — as if continuing a talk that began before the camera
Bullets (3–5)     each one line, in their own words: one idea or one example
Closing (last 4)  an open question to the audience or a frank confession — never "follow" or "save"
```
1. **No manufactured hook.** Banned: "99% don't know", "don't do this", "now I'll tell you the secret". The opening is ordinary: "Something happened yesterday that made me think…", "There's a thing I do every time and regret."
2. **No delivery marks.** The rhythm comes from the words.
3. **A bullet is one line and starts with an action or a situation, not a title:** "I tried recording with no script and lost my thread" — not "The no-script recording experience".
4. **At least one real example from their own life** (ask for it if they haven't given one).
5. **The need is there but hidden** — usually Connection or Growth. Still write `Need: Connection` as line 1.
6. **The closing is an open question** ("How do you do it?", "Am I wrong?") — comments drive distribution here, not saves.
7. **Length 40–70 s** — a bit longer than rhythm mode, because closeness takes time.
8. For the prompter it shows as bullets: one idea per line, never paragraphs.

Filming note: one locked shot, no cuts, natural light, eyes on the lens like it's a person. If they stumble, they keep going; no retakes.

Example:
```
Need: Connection
Yesterday I posted a video that took 3 hours to edit, my brother watched it, said "nice"… and closed it.
- First I thought: he doesn't see the effort behind it.
- Then I asked myself: should he? People see the result, not the road.
- Since then I ask before every video: if a stranger saw this, what would they take from it?
- And when I can't find a clear answer, I don't post it.
How do you know a video is worth posting? Tell me honestly.
```

## 9. Voice and dialect
Write in the **owner's own voice and dialect**, not formal textbook language: short sentences, a friend talking, never a speech. Ask once, on the first script, which dialect or register they want (if the language has several) and keep it. Digits are Western (1 2 3), even inside timings. No mixed-in foreign words except product names.
